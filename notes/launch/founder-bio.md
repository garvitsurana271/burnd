He's fluent in React, TypeScript, Python, and the Claude API. Burnd's local-first architecture means zero data leaves the user's machine, with a public anonymization spec and CI tests asserting no credentials leak.

```
Burnd is the first product under Garvit's "ChangeLife" revenue umbrella — his attempt at building real software revenue before starting college in 2027.
```

## Photo guidance (for when someone asks for one)

- No fake corporate headshot. You're 16 and you should look 16.
- If you don't have a good photo, use a plain colored circle with your initials "GS" on the landing page. Many indie founders use this — it's fine.
- If you DO take a photo, make it: (a) good lighting, (b) neutral background, (c) a slight smile, not a posed grin, (d) reasonable camera angle (not selfie stick). Your school uniform is fine if that's what you have.
- For medium-quality press needs, ask a friend or family member to take one with a real phone camera.
- For high-quality press needs, outsource to a local photographer for ₹500-1000. Keep the receipt, claim it as a business expense when you eventually incorporate.

## Voice / positioning notes

When people write about you, they'll try to describe you in their own words. You can nudge them:

- **DO call yourself:** a builder, an indie founder, a student who ships, a Claude Code user first and a tool-maker second
- **DO NOT call yourself:** a startup founder (too premature), an entrepreneur (too corporate), a "young innovator" (condescending), a prodigy (cringe), "the next Pieter Levels" (let other people say this if they want)
- **DO lean into:** the India angle, the 16-year-old angle, the honest real-data angle, the bootstrap angle
- **DO NOT lean into:** the "disrupting" angle, the "AI is the future" angle, the "I'll be a unicorn" angle

Keep your bio honest. People can smell performance.
